The Big Picture · What All Data Says Together
  • One rule governs all budgets: Utilization is at 80%. Do not increase daily budgets anywhere — SH or STI. Only trigger a budget increase city-by-city when utilization crosses 90% consistently. First candidate: Navi Mumbai (both SH and STI underfunded and leading).
  • SH and STI face completely different competitive landscapes. SH competitors are clinic/specialist rivals — the lever is bid and organic moat (reviews). STI competitors are diagnostic labs (drsafehands, orangehealth, 1mg) — the lever is keyword surgery (remove testing-intent terms). Mixing these strategies is a waste of spend.
  • Navi Mumbai is the highest-ROI market in the entire portfolio — SH CPL ₹151 (best), Loc→Lead 28% (best), STI IS 75.73% and abs top 40% (only STI city we dominate). Combined daily spend is only ₹1,300 (SH ₹1,000 + STI ₹300). This is the clearest underinvestment signal in the data.
  • Three campaigns have critically low optimization scores and must be fixed before any bid changes: Mumbai SH (58.5%), Mumbai STI (56.9%), Hyderabad STI (51.4%), and the worst in portfolio — Chennai STI (49.8%). These are structural, not strategic issues.
  • STI campaigns are buying lab impressions, not clinic impressions. Our STI abs top rate is 4–10% in 5 of 6 cities despite decent IS, because drsafehands (a home testing lab) consistently bids at position 1 for testing queries. Adding testing-intent negatives across all STI campaigns this week is the single highest-leverage action available.
  • The Navi Mumbai STI 7-group structure is the proof of concept: 7 tightly focused ad groups → IS 75.73%, abs top 40%. Other cities have 13–17 groups on tiny budgets (₹300–550/day). Consolidating Bangalore (17→7–8 groups) and Mumbai (13→6–7 groups) will improve QS without any cost increase.
  • Organic moat (GMB reviews) determines Loc→Lead conversion — 15% in Mumbai (weak moat) vs 28% in Navi Mumbai (strong moat). Paid spend into weak GMB profiles converts at half the rate. PRM review-building is not a nice-to-have; it is a paid performance lever.
  • The SH rivals are clinics you CAN outcompete on reviews. The STI rivals are labs you CANNOT outcompete on testing queries. This asymmetry means SH strategy is about building a better product/profile; STI strategy is about escaping the wrong auction entirely.

STI landing pages may be the root cause of low QS, not just keywords
The STI campaigns' low optimization scores (49–57%) are partly a landing page mismatch problem. If our ads show for "STD near me" queries but the landing page says "sexual health clinic" or "ED treatment," Google's system sees irrelevance and cuts the QS. The fix is two-part: (1) remove testing-intent keywords so we stop showing for those queries, AND (2) ensure STI landing pages use the exact terms patients search ("STI treatment", "STD doctor", "STD consultation") rather than clinical jargon. This is a landing page copy task, not just a keyword task.
Ad copy relevance is a hidden QS lever
QS has 3 components: expected CTR, ad relevance, and landing page experience. We've talked about keywords (expected CTR) and landing pages (landing page experience). Ad relevance is the missing one. If our STI ad headlines say "Allo Health | Sexual Health Clinic" but the query is "STD doctor near me Hyderabad", the relevance score is low. Review and update STI ad copy to include the exact treatment terms — "STD Doctor · Same Day Appointment," "STI Treatment · Expert Consultation" etc. Each ad group should have a headline matching the group's intent.
