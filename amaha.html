Key Competitive Signals
🚫 No web self-serve booking — ALL routes through phone/app

Clicking Book on any profile triggers a care-team modal: "Call +91 2071 171 501 or open the app." Zero direct slot selection on web. A competitor with click-to-book wins every SERP conversion battle.

📉 Senior supply is window dressing — they don't control it

15+ year clinicians: 46% fully booked, avg 163 open slots vs 460 platform median. These are independent practitioners who listed for visibility. Amaha can't guarantee access to their best names.

💰 Price is purely experience-based — no other differentiation

Every 5-year band adds ₹500–900. No degree premium, no specialisation premium, no city premium. 99% charge the same for online vs in-person. Mechanical pricing = easy to undercut selectively.

📍 Mumbai is effectively uncontested — 24 clinicians, lowest density

75 in Bengaluru vs 24 in Mumbai. Slots/clinician in Mumbai = 321 vs 567 in Gurugram. Anyone wanting to own the Mumbai mental health market faces minimal Amaha resistance.

📈 82% supply ramp in 13 weeks — a big Q4 demand push is coming

Slots jump from ~5K/wk (Jul 27) to ~9.1K/wk (Oct 19). You don't build that inventory without a corresponding demand campaign. Get brand visibility in Sep before their spend hits.

🧒 CF (Children First) — premium-priced, thin, not web-bookable

54 CF clinicians, avg fee ₹3,500–₹4,767 (highest on platform). Most require referral assessment, not direct web booking. Pediatric mental health = high-value, low-competition segment.

City opportunity matrix

Mumbai = biggest opportunity

24 clinicians for India's largest metro. Lowest slots/dr (321 vs 567 Gurugram). Patients likely wait or go elsewhere. A well-supplied competitor could own this market in 6–12 months.

Bengaluru = high switching cost

75 in-person clinicians, 32K slots over 3 months. Deeply embedded. To compete here requires volume play, not quality niche. Online-only is more viable than offline head-to-head.

Delhi/NCR split — GGN has spare capacity

Gurugram (21 drs, 567 avg slots) clearly has room — fewer patients than capacity. Delhi (43 drs, 497 avg) is more balanced. NCR = a real battleground.

CAC Signal — Phone-routed bookings = expensive ops

Every booking requires a care-team member to take an inbound call, triage, match clinician, and schedule. At volume (4,000 sessions/wk), this is a massive ops cost — likely ₹300–600+ per booking in people cost alone.

Implication:

Their contribution margin per session is lower than fee suggests. A self-serve platform competitor with same fees has 15–25% margin advantage before any other efficiency.
